The primary objective of a donut chart is to compare each group’s contribution to the whole.

It does it by representing the distribution of a total amount across different levels of a categorical variable by dividing a circle into radial slices.

  

**Use a donut chart to analyze each group's contribution to the whole rather than comparing groups directly**

If comparing groups is the focus, consider using a different chart type. Differentiating between slices can be challenging, even when sorted by size.

**Include percentage annotations in the graph or the legend**

It's hard to determine precise proportions from pie charts, outside of fractions like 1/2 (50%), 1/3 (33%), and 1/4 (25%), making annotations essential.

**Order your slices**

A logical slice order helps readers grasp the plot easily. Start with the largest slice, then move to the smallest. If categories have a natural order, follow that for better clarity.

**Start from the top**

**Display only 5 categories or group small slices in neutral gray**

Pie charts with many slices are difficult to read. The smallest slices may not be visible, and using enough distinct colors can be challenging. If no solution is effective, consider using a different chart type if you have over five categories.
