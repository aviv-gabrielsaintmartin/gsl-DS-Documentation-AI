## Usage

Chip groups are dynamic, interactive collections of chips that allow users to filter content, toggle options, or make multiple selections within a related set, providing an efficient way to organize and manage complex choices or actions.

### Related components

**Component**

**Usage**

Chip group

[Chip](https://zeroheight.com/626199550/p/32f686-chip)

Chips are dynamic, clickable standalone components that allow user interaction, such as selecting, filtering, or removing items.

[Button](https://zeroheight.com/626199550/p/97e03c-button)

Buttons trigger actions.

[Tag](https://zeroheight.com/626199550/p/28d2fb-tag)

Tags are non-interactive components used to display fixed information such as labels, categories, or statuses. They are purely informational and do not allow user interaction.

## Content

**Filter Chips**

Use concise, descriptive labels that clearly communicate the purpose of the filter. Keep labels consistent across similar filters.

  

**Input Chips**

Write labels that are specific and relevant to the input or selection being represented. Maintain brevity while ensuring that users can easily identify the context or purpose of the input.

  

**Action Chips**

Use action-oriented labels that clearly indicate the task being performed. Keep labels short and concise. The label should always lead with an action verb that encourages action, in the infinitive tense. To provide enough context to our users, use the {verb} + {noun} content formula.

  

Use sentence case without punctuation. Try to keep it to 2-3 words and about 20-30 characters in English.

For more information on content guidelines, please refer to the [UX Writing principles](https://zeroheight.com/626199550/p/324518-intro).
